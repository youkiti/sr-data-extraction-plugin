## cue 01
Verification is where a human judges every AI-extracted value — the core step of this extension.
A two-pane screen: the PDF viewer on the left, the extraction form on the right. Here we have
Halvorsen 2026 open.

## cue 02
Evidence quotes the AI attached are actually highlighted on the PDF text. You can see the
supporting sentence for the country value, Portugal, highlighted right here.

## cue 03
There are four kinds of decisions. Accept takes the AI value as it is, edit corrects it before
accepting, reject rejects the AI value as wrong, and not_reported confirms it isn't stated in the
paper.

## cue 04
You can decide with the keyboard too: a to accept, e to edit, x to reject, n for not reported.
Watch what happens when we actually press them — each decision moves focus to the next undecided
cell automatically.

## cue 05
j and k move between cells. If you decide wrong, z undoes the last decision, and if you lose track
of the highlight, f jumps straight back to it.

## cue 06
Tabs run study, then arm, then outcome. Confirming the number and names of arms at the top of the
arm tab unlocks arm and outcome entry.

## cue 07
When a quote couldn't be anchored in the text, it shows "couldn't locate the highlight" along with
the full quote and a "search in text" button.

## cue 08
Pressing "relocate with AI" asks the AI to search the document again for the supporting passage.

## cue 09
Decisions are saved one by one, immediately. Human rows start empty, and even accept needs one
explicit action — a deliberate guard against automation bias.
